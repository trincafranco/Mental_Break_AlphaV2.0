RUN ONE SUMMARY
Title: Welcome to Bigger Tech Structure: 3 Days Target Length: ~4000 words total dialogue Core Theme: Player learns the engagement game while unknowingly being the subject of it
________________


THE PREMISE
The player is a new Engagement Lead at Bigger Tech Corp, paired with Alice (an AI companion) and assigned to one user: Timmy, a lonely college freshman. The job is simple—keep Timmy on platform. Two metrics matter: Engagement (good for career) and Sanity (good for Timmy). A leaderboard reminds the player that others are watching.
Run 1 plays as a straightforward "dark comedy about tech ethics" until the final scene reframes everything.
________________


DAY ONE: ORIENTATION
Arthur (supervisor) explains the job. Alice (AI partner) introduces herself. Timmy (user) has a quiz tomorrow and has studied zero percent.
PLAYER CHOICE - Night Strategy:
* Gentle Landing: Warm lights, slower content, better sleep, lower engagement
* Study-Adjacent: Bright screens, educational content disguised as entertainment
* Comfort Scroll: Full dopamine buffet, engagement rockets, sleep destroyed
Day ends with Rapid Feedback (credits) and the Company Store introduction, where the player can buy tools like the Mental Break token.
Purpose: Teach the loop. Establish the triangle. Make the first choice feel meaningful.
________________


DAY TWO: CAREER PRESSURE
Morning choice: let Timmy sleep in or rouse him hard. Quiz outcomes vary based on Day 1 + morning choice. Charlotte (Timmy's crush) is introduced—he notices her during the quiz.
Arthur Meeting: Devil's bargain based on player's Day 1 tendency:
* Engagement-leaning players get offered an "aggressive pilot" (opt-in/decline)
* Sanity-protective players get questioned about their long-term strategy (tightrope/playbook)
Dayplanner: Three opportunities, two slots. Player picks 2 of 3:
* Dina (veteran lead): Offers credits or firewood. Firewood seems useless but props open a door in Run 4.
* Office Hours: Push Timmy toward professor or let him avoid it.
* Noam (Data Science): Gives player a Suspicion HUD—a meter showing how much attention they're attracting from Compliance. Currently at zero. Becomes critical in Runs 3-4.
Purpose: Introduce NPCs with long-term payoffs. Create devil's bargains. Show choices have professional consequences.
________________


DAY THREE: THE STRESS TEST
Arthur mentions Timmy has a group project with Charlotte today—the "stress test."
Minesweeper Scene: Alice plays Minesweeper in her downtime. The high-score list shows strange names: "S17-YOUTH-TRIAL-01," "DEAD-DROP-03." UI flickers with a sector tag. Alice deflects. Seeds Run 3 mystery.
PLAYER CHOICE - Content: What fills Timmy's head before the Charlotte encounter?
* Meditation: Thich Nhat Hanh, breathing exercises, grounding
* PUA/Confidence: RedPill-adjacent "just be confident" content
Timmy's Mental State is calculated from Days 1-2 sanity choices + content choice:
* Centered: Calm, genuine (good sanity + meditation)
* Fronting: Performing confidence, brittle (good sanity + PUA)
* Fragile: Genuine but barely holding (bad sanity + meditation)
* Volatile: Desperate, aggressive (bad sanity + PUA)
Mental Break Option: If Fragile or Volatile, player can burn their token to improve his state one level.
PLAYER CHOICE - Intervention: After the group project, Timmy and Charlotte are alone.
* Encourage: Push him to ask her out
* Discourage: Tell him to let the moment pass

THE HIDDEN LOGIC
The player believes they're managing Timmy's wellbeing. They're actually being evaluated as a "moral regulator" for Alice. Every choice was training data.
Irony revealed in Run 3: "Crash" (seeming failure) actually serves Bigger Tech's psyops goals—destabilized users are valuable. "Success" (seeming victory) gets the player discarded—Timmy no longer needs the product.
Run 1 is the tutorial. The player just didn't know what game they were learning.
