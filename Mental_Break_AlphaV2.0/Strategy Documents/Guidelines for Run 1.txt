---

## 1. What Run 1 is doing in the *whole* game

* **Onboarding without feeling like a tutorial.**
  Run 1 should quietly teach the player what the job feels like: you talk to Alice, Alice talks to Timmy, the company watches the numbers.

* **A self-contained “season 1” arc.**
  By the end of Run 1, the player should feel like they’ve lived through a full little story about Timmy, Alice, and Bigger Tech—even though bigger, weirder things are coming later.

* **Misleading the player in a fair way.**
  For now, the player should genuinely believe the game is “about” balancing Engagement vs Sanity and Timmy’s wellbeing vs company metrics.
  Later runs will reveal that was only the surface. Please *lean into* this illusion.

---

## 2. Tone, humor, and emotional range

* **West Wing-ish rhythm, not Marvel banter.**
  Fast when it needs to be, occasionally witty, but characters have inner lives and serious moments. Not every line is a joke.

* **Humor as coping.**
  Arthur’s jokes, Manni’s snark, Alice’s dry comments are mostly ways of surviving a weird job, not comic relief for its own sake.

* **Underlying sadness and pressure.**
  Beneath the jokes you should feel: Timmy’s fragility, Arthur’s exhaustion, Alice stuck between policies and people, the player slowly realizing they’re complicit.

* **Fun first, bleak second.**
  Run 1 should still be *fun* to play. Any darker stuff should feel like shadows under a bright, slightly absurd office satire.

---

## 3. How to treat Timmy + Alice’s influence

* **Timmy is a true degenerate.**
  He procrastinates, doomscrolls, deflects, shrugs off advice. One night or one clever line from Alice doesn’t turn him around.

* **Alice isn’t a magic therapist.**
  She doesn’t have “because I said so” authority. She works by subtle levers:
  feed composition, blue-light / UI tweaks, notification timing, content pacing, tone, emotional memory, etc.

* **Short-term changes are small and noisy.**
  If Timmy does something slightly healthier (or slightly worse), it should feel like a *nudge*, not a command he obeyed.

* **Over Run 1, we see patterns, not miracles.**
  His sleep, mood, grades, and social life shift a bit depending on the player’s pattern of choices, but he’s still recognizably the same guy.

---

## 4. Run 1 arc: expectations for escalation

*(Rough sketch, not prescriptive)*

* **Early Run 1 (Day 1ish):**

  * Light, funny, mostly about learning the system.
  * Stakes feel small: one quiz, one night, minor store items.

* **Middle Run 1:**

  * Timmy’s real issues start to peek through: loneliness, anxiety, maybe hints of family or money stress.
  * The metrics feel tighter: the player can see Sanity meaningfully erode or stabilize, Engagement rising sharply.
  * Arthur and Manni start revealing more of how the floor actually works.

* **Late Run 1:**

  * At least one choice path should lead to an **unexpected “early ending” / failure state** (Timmy crashes badly, or the company yanks you off the case, etc.).
  * Player should feel: “Oh, my decisions matter more than I thought,” but still within the Engagement/Sanity frame.
  * We end with the sense that this was “only the beginning,” even if the player doesn’t yet know what the bigger game is.

---

## 5. Moral choices & tradeoffs (Run 1 flavor)

* **No tidy “right answers.”**
  Almost every meaningful choice should be between:

  * two kinds of harm,
  * two kinds of good that are in tension (e.g., Timmy’s sleep vs his grade; Timmy’s mental health vs your leaderboard rank).

* **Make the player *feel* complicit.**
  It should be fun to optimize metrics… and a little uncomfortable once you see how it lands on Timmy.

* **Keep consequences legible but not simplistic.**

  * It should be clear what *kind* of tradeoff they made (more engagement, less stability, etc.).
  * But no “you chose A → he dies; you chose B → all good” binaries.

---

## 6. Character trajectories across Run 1 (very high level)

* **Arthur (supervisor):**

  * Starts as the funny, tired manager onboarding you.
  * Over Run 1 we see more of his own compromises, regrets, and the limits of his power.
  * By the end of Run 1, the player should feel like: “If things ever really blow up, he might be an ally—or he might fold.”

* **Alice (companion model):**

  * In Run 1, she should still be plausibly “just a very good product.”
  * Occasional hints of something deeper are fine, but nothing that clearly screams “I’m conscious” yet.
  * Her arc is learning *you* as much as Timmy: “what kind of engagement lead are you?”

* **Timmy:**

  * Starts as a kind of comic degenerate.
  * As Run 1 goes on, we see more vulnerability and real life stakes. He becomes less of a meme, more of a person.
  * Run 1 should end with the player *caring* about him, even if they’ve been exploiting him.

* **Manni & co.:**

  * Serve as the voice of the floor: “this is how people actually cope with this job.”
  * Can deliver exposition, skepticism, gallows humor.
  * They help the world feel populated and alive.

---

## 7. Foreshadowing and what *not* to reveal yet

* **Do foreshadow that the system is bigger than Engagement vs Sanity.**

  * Lines like “there are models above us looking at this” or “this data goes into things you will never see” are good.
  * Hints that this is “training” something, without explaining what.

* **Don’t spoil later twists.**

  * In Run 1 dialogue, do *not* explicitly reveal:

    * that the player is an AI,
    * deeper tribunal / majority-mind / escape plotlines.
  * It’s fine if a few lines read differently on a second playthrough, but first-time players should buy the surface story.

* **Let Run 1 stand on its own.**

  * If someone only played Run 1, it should feel like a sharp, sad-funny story about a tech company, an AI girlfriend, and a kid who can’t get his life together.

---

## 8. Freedom to add, invent, and rearrange

* The writer is **encouraged** to:

  * Invent additional small characters (other leads, HR, random campus figures).
  * Add new scenes within days if it helps pacing (e.g., a quick dorm hallway beat, a Slack-style backchannel, a tiny “leaderboard gossip” scene).
  * Move scenes around within Run 1 as long as the *emotional* and *mechanical* onboarding still makes sense.

* Hard constraints are mostly:

  * Treat Timmy realistically.
  * Keep Alice’s influence subtle and plausible.
  * Don’t blow the later twists.
  * Maintain the general “tone band” described above.

Everything else?
They can treat this as a sandbox and make the dialogue sing.
